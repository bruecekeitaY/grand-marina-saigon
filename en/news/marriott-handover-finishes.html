Inside Grand Marina's Marriott Handover: Finishes & Appliances, Room by Room

What does "fully fitted to Marriott standard" actually deliver? This guide breaks the apartment down room by room — floors, Boffi kitchens, Miele appliances, Toto bathrooms and Daikin VRV — so you know exactly what's already installed when you take the keys.

When buyers hear that Grand Marina Saigon is "fully fitted to Marriott standard," the most practical question is always the same: what is actually inside the apartment, which brands are used, and how much more will I need to spend before I can move in or lease it out? As Vietnam's first Marriott & JW Marriott Branded Residences, developed by Masterise Homes at Ba Son in District 1, the handover specification is a core part of the value here — not an optional extra. This article walks through each area of the home and lists the material and appliance brands the project has published.

What does "fully fitted to Marriott standard" really mean?

It means a fully fitted handover — the apartment arrives with floors, kitchen, appliances, bathrooms and ducted air-conditioning already installed to a single premium standard, so you only add loose furniture before living in or leasing.

Unlike the bare-shell or basic handovers common in many projects, "fully fitted to Marriott standard" means the most expensive, hardest-to-change elements — the kitchen system, integrated appliances, flooring, bathrooms and the AC system — are pre-installed to one consistent specification overseen by operator Marriott International. This matters because, to understand why a branded apartment is priced differently, it helps to first read What are branded residences? before comparing it with ordinary developments.

Floors and doors: the foundation of a branded apartment

Grand Marina apartments are handed over with marble and engineered-wood floors by zone, plus a double-glazed imported glazing system.

The flooring is split sensibly by function: living areas, the kitchen and the entrance hall typically use marble for a sense of luxury and easy cleaning, while bedrooms use engineered wood for warmth underfoot and a quieter feel. The doors and glass partitions use imported double-glazed glass, which dampens the urban noise of District 1 and reduces heat gain — important for a riverfront facade and city views. These are exactly the items a bare-shell buyer would otherwise have to pay heavily for and spend months fitting out.

Poggenpohl/Boffi kitchens and Miele/Gaggenau appliances

The kitchen is handed over with cabinetry by Poggenpohl or Boffi, paired with integrated kitchen appliances from Miele or Gaggenau.

This is the detail that impresses buyers most on a show-unit visit. Poggenpohl (Germany) and Boffi (Italy) are two of the world's leading premium kitchen brands, regularly specified in international luxury apartments. The accompanying appliances — induction hob, oven, extractor and integrated fridge — come from Miele or Gaggenau, names known for durability and prestige. Toto/Duravit/Hansgrohe bathrooms and the Daikin VRV system

Bathrooms are handed over with Toto and Duravit sanitaryware and Hansgrohe fittings, while the whole apartment uses a ducted Daikin VRV air-conditioning system.

The bathroom is where attention to detail shows most clearly. The package uses brands trusted in five-star hotels:

  • Toto (Japan) — WCs, basins and advanced washlet technology.
  • Duravit (Germany) — premium designer sanitary ceramics.
  • Hansgrohe (Germany) — durable, elegant showers, taps and accessories.

This is paired with a central Daikin VRV ducted system throughout the apartment, delivering even cooling, quiet operation and a cleaner look than ordinary wall-mounted units. The apartment also comes with an app-based smart-home system, letting you control lighting, blinds and air-conditioning from your phone.

How does the handover standard affect value and rental?

An apartment delivered with brand-matched hard finishes shortens time-to-occupancy and supports positioning toward the premium rental segment.

Because the hardest and most expensive work is already done, buyers usually only need to add loose furniture (sofa, beds, dining table, decor) before moving in or letting it out. According to research houses such as Knight Frank and Savills (recent market reference), branded residences are typically priced 25–35% above comparable non-branded apartments — partly thanks to this very handover standard and the internationally operated services. This is a market reference, not a promise; actual results depend on the project, timing and policy.
