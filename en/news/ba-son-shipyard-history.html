Ba Son History: From a 200-Year Shipyard to Prime District 1 Land

The 200-year journey of the Ba Son shipyard on the Saigon River — and why the last large riverside plot in the heart of District 1 is so hard to replace.

  1. Home
  2. News
  3. Ba Son History

Long before it became home to Vietnam's first branded residences, Ba Son was a name tied to Saigon's industrial and maritime history for more than two centuries. This article tells that story — and explains why this depth of history is part of what makes the land where Grand Marina Saigon stands such a rare piece of prime District 1 real estate.

Where is Ba Son and why is it famous?

Ba Son is a riverside plot on the Saigon River at No. 02 Ton Duc Thang, Ben Nghe Ward, District 1 — once home to one of Vietnam's oldest shipyards and now the site of Grand Marina Saigon.

Ba Son sits on a curve of the Saigon River, facing the Thu Thiem peninsula and just minutes from District 1's administrative and commercial core. For generations of Saigon residents, "Ba Son" is not merely a place name but a symbol of labour, engineering and modern history. Because it has always been bound to the river and its docks, the area holds a geographic position that is extremely hard to find again in the central core today.

How long has the Ba Son shipyard existed?

The shipyard at Ba Son carries roughly 200 years of history, making it one of the oldest mechanical and maritime industrial sites in Vietnam.

Originating as a military naval workshop on the banks of the Saigon River in the early 19th century, the area gradually grew into a large-scale shipbuilding and repair facility across several eras. Through different periods of history, Ba Son remained a hub of mechanical engineering, dry docks and skilled workers — an "industrial lung" of the city right next to its centre.

Because of that depth, the name Ba Son is etched into the urban memory of many Saigon generations. When the shipyard was relocated and the land was repurposed, it became one of the most valuable and closely watched riverside land banks in District 1.

Why is Ba Son called the "last riverside plot" in District 1?

Ba Son is often described as the last large riverside plot remaining in the central core of District 1, because land directly on the Saigon River in this area is almost entirely built out.

The core of District 1 has long been filled with existing buildings, street grids and developments. A large, contiguous parcel with Saigon River frontage and located right next to the central business district is nearly impossible to recreate. That is why market observers often describe Ba Son with words like "one of a kind" or "irreplaceable".

  • Saigon River frontage — only about 200 m from the water.
  • Facing the Thu Thiem New Urban Area (657 ha) across the river, with Thu Thiem Bridge 2 already completed.
  • Ba Son Metro station (Ben Thanh – Suoi Tien line, now operating) is only about 250 m away.
  • Within 1 km are the Opera House, Nguyen Hue walking street and Ben Thanh Market.

Together, these factors create a location that new projects find very hard to match, simply because comparable land in this area has run out. This scarcity of location is also something buyers weigh when considering long-term value — though actual outcomes still depend on many market factors.

From shipyard to Grand Marina Saigon: how was the land transformed?

After the shipyard was relocated, the Ba Son land was redeveloped into a complex of Marriott and JW Marriott branded residences — the Grand Marina Saigon project developed by Masterise Homes.

Grand Marina Saigon is Vietnam's first branded residences project, developed by Masterise Homes and serviced by Marriott International under a 20-year operating contract. The project comprises four towers — Lake, Lagoon, Cove and Sea — across the Marriott and JW Marriott brands. All phases have now been handed over, residents have moved in and the Marriott concierge team is operating; an active secondary resale market has also developed in 2024–2026.

In other words, the land that once echoed with the sound of machinery and ship horns now hosts apartments finished to Marriott standards beside the Saigon River. If you're curious about the hotel-operated residence model, read What are branded residences? to understand the concept.

For buyers, the history and scarcity of the Ba Son land create a location story that is hard to copy — a factor usually weighed alongside price, legal status and real living or rental demand.

A riverside position next to the Metro and the District 1 core carries not only symbolic value but also affects real demand. For owner-occupiers, it means convenience and views; for rental investors, it means appeal to expatriate professionals and senior executives working downtown. How does the location story affect residence value?

A one-of-a-kind riverside location combined with the branded-residence model are two factors the market tends to price above ordinary apartments, but actual value still depends on timing and policy.

According to market research firms such as Knight Frank and Savills, branded residences worldwide are typically priced around 25–35% higher than comparable non-branded apartments (a market reference figure from recent years). When the "branded" attribute is combined with a historic, scarce riverside location like Ba Son, the location story becomes part of how the market views long-term value.

This is not a guarantee of returns, however. Actual outcomes depend on the specific project, the timing of any transaction and the policies in place at each stage. Buyers should review the legal documents and the price list for each phase carefully before deciding, and note the ownership rules: Vietnamese buyers receive a long-term pink book, while foreigners hold 50-year ownership (renewable under Vietnamese law) capped at 30% of units per building.
